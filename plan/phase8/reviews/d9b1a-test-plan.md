### Safe-start deltas to pin

The rule is always the closing send's **exclusive end + 1 sample**. A normal payload EOB already at least two samples long keeps the existing `+1` boundary. The expected boundary moves one sample later for each old short close: a 1-sample Kernel burst now closes as payload+zero (old front `start+2`, new `start+3`); a held-tail close is tail+zero (old `cursor+1`, new `cursor+2`); and a no-tail or failed-send close is two zeros after its cursor (old `cursor+2`, new `cursor+3`). These are device fronts; padding must not change the Kernel record's payload count or end.

## Amendments after Sol (2026-10-29)

Sol's five findings are adopted. These refine the sequences above; they do not widen this stage into R2′, #71, underflow continuation, or receive-side D9b-2.

### T3 ordering and ownership

Keep untimed TX configuration in `prepare`. T3 means that, for each streamer generation, a timed TX DSP value is issued only after that chain's flush-ack read completes or its bounded wait expires and records a `tx_flush` rejection; the flush data send precedes experiment data on that path. It is not a FIFO drain: the untimed flush does not consume already queued timed DSP values. State this limitation in UR-22 and keep KL-12 from claiming more. The three openers read the ack within their existing ownership: `arm` before workers start; from-none after any existing `reading` ends while it retains `FromNone`; uhd-tx on reopen while it retains `Owner`. Do not call `begin_read`, and do not hold `streams` over device calls or the wait. Explicitly test the `begin_shutdown_read` interaction, which ignores the holder.

For each open route, add a barrier test that withholds the flush ack, attempts a concurrent timed TX release, and observes that no timed TX DSP write occurs until the ack arrives or the recorded timeout expires. Include simultaneous shutdown reading and verify serialized report ownership. Keep the existing separate checks that untimed `prepare` configuration stays in prepare and experiment data follows the flush.

### Sol Finding 1 — TimeError attribution (P1)

Treat a report tick as the radio's current time, not the rejected packet's scheduled time. Never attribute by testing whether the tick falls in a pending burst range, and never consume another burst's pending target merely because its range contains the tick. If a report cannot be attributed uniquely, preserve the raw `device_async` row and leave targets unclaimed. Extend the report tests with: A's error after A's range; that same error during B's range; an error after A was abandoned; and a report with no tick. Assert one event only when attribution is unique, and that B remains pending in the ambiguous cases.

### Sol Finding 3 — flush failure, deadline, and identity (P2)

Use one total device-clock deadline for T3 and bound every `tx_async` read by remaining time; repeated immediate read errors and unrelated reports must still make progress and terminate at that deadline. Do not reuse the shutdown drain loop. Test flush sends accepting zero or one sample and send error; repeated read errors and unrelated reports; acknowledgements from every relevant channel; device loss separately from a missing ack; Stop during the wait; and a late flush ack that cannot retire an experiment burst correlation. A bounded missing ack records `rejected { call: "tx_flush", stream, error }` and permits the stream to continue; device loss remains `DEVICE_LOST` and ends device use.

Before the next timed burst on that stream, perform a T3 flush (send and ack wait under the same deadline/ownership rules) before its timed data. Assert the next burst is not treated as safely initialized from the attempted close alone.
