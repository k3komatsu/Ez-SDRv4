## 0. Reading the brief: what the plan assumes (the orchestrator may overrule any line)

1. **Names.** `const T3_ATTEMPTS: usize = 3` and `const OPEN_FLUSH_CONFIRMED: bool = false` in `core.rs`. `tx_flush_locked(stream, channels, attempt: Option<Attempt>) -> TxFlushResult` with `struct Attempt { number: usize, not_before: i64 }`: `None` is **today's function, unchanged** (the legacy attempt, §2.5); `Some(Attempt { number: n, not_before })` is the strict attempt `n` (1-based); in the text below `Some(n)` abbreviates it with `not_before = i64::MIN` (no old front) unless a test names a value. `TxFlushResult` gains `problem: Option<String>` (the text the loop keeps and the row carries); it keeps `acknowledged`, `ack_tick` and `stopped`, so the legacy callers and the 14 existing flush tests (§5) are untouched. New: `struct Confirmed { ack_tick: i64 }` and `fn open_tx_confirmed_locked(k, set, channels, not_before: i64) -> Result<Confirmed, DeviceError>` (the loop); `reopen_tx_streamer(k, set, not_before)` gains the same parameter, which `reopen_streamer` fills from the book's old front (§3); the test entry `open_tx_confirmed(k, set, not_before)` is the one the core-level tests call (`i64::MIN` unless stated). `Ok(Confirmed)` is a confirmed T3 and nothing else; an exhausted loop is `Err(DeviceError::failed("UR-22: the recovery flush was not confirmed after 3 attempts: <last problem>"))`, not lost. Only `core.rs` and two expressions of `tx.rs` (the `not_before` computation and `tx.rs:816`) see the types. The implementer may rename; the observable contract is what the tests pin.
2. **A strict attempt is confirmed** when its pre-send time read succeeded, its send was whole (`Ok(4)`), and every channel position has a `BurstAck` whose tick is `Some(t)` with `t >= floor`, all read within the attempt's 100 ms. **`floor = max(P, not_before)`** (N6): `P` is the device time (`device.time_now()`) read immediately before that attempt's send; `not_before` is the root-tick instant of the book's old front (`TxBook`'s device front, `staging.rs:597`, converted with the stream's clock: `clock.try_instant(front)`, `core.rs:245`), or `i64::MIN` when there is no old front, the front is of another clock domain, or the stream has no clock. `ack_tick` is the largest such tick. Any other end is unconfirmed.
   **Residual of the floor (N6; hardware parts INFERRED).** (i) The old front is the front of the last close that *succeeded* (`advance_front` only moves on a whole EOB send, `staging.rs:629-644`), so the burst whose EOB failed — the usual reason for a recovery — is **not** in it. Its own `BurstAck`, if its EOB was delivered and the burst ends after `P`, carries a tick ≥ `P` and is still accepted as the attempt's (Fable's sequence B in the one case the front does not bound). The bound is `P` alone there; closing it needs the abandoned burst's cursor, which this plan does not pass (an option for the orchestrator, §10 item 6). (ii) A genuine T3 ACK of a flush queued behind a still-playing old burst lands about four samples after that burst's end, while the old front is the end plus `g ≥ 15` samples on the transmit grid (`design/18-uhd-radio.md:257`, `closed_short_gap`, `staging.rs:86-89`): it can fall **below** the floor. The attempt is then unconfirmed and the next one, 100 ms later, is past the front: the cost is one attempt, never a false confirmation. (iii) On the fake with a manual clock the same happens whenever a test sets an artificial old front ahead of device time (§5 audits every such test).
3. **A non-whole send ends its attempt at once** (decision 1): `Ok(0)`, `Ok(n<4)` and a non-lost `Err` return before any `tx_async` read and before the 100 ms wait. **The pre-send time read (N3, decided by the orchestrator):** the attempt's deadline `until = host.now() + 100 ms` is computed **before** the read; a failed read (not lost) is retried with a 1 ms back-off (`host.sleep(min(until − now, 1 ms))`), the tolerance `control.rs:650-660` already gives a non-lost time-read failure (`FAILING_FOR`, `control.rs:158`); each iteration checks `is_lost()` and `Provider::stop` first, as the ACK wait does (`core.rs:1245-1249`), and a test-only `probe("t3_time_read")` fires after each failed read. A read that succeeds before `until` sets `P`; the send then gets the rest of the window as its timeout, as today. A read still failing at `until` ends the attempt **without sending** (without a floor nothing can be attributed; a send would only add a flush nobody can confirm): one `rejected` row `flush time read failed: <error>` with the attempt number, no event. A lost error at the time read or at the send publishes `DEVICE_LOST` (as today, `core.rs:1235`/`device_failed`) and ends the loop.
4. **Per-attempt record and events.** Each unconfirmed strict attempt writes one `rejected { call: "tx_flush", stream, error, attempt }` row (additive field; `rejected` rows are free-form, `core.rs:1016`). A confirmed attempt leaves its `async` rows (`tx_flush: true`, `core.rs:1204-1208`) and no `rejected` row. **No strict attempt emits an event** (F2): a non-lost failed send is a row only. The legacy attempt keeps `device_failed` (event `tx_flush`) exactly as today (M2).
5. **Messages.** Recovery failure: `UR-22: the recovery flush was not confirmed after 3 attempts: …` (the tests match on `after 3 attempts` and the last attempt's problem). The open-time texts (UR-13/UR-22) land with the flip (§2.5).
6. **Locks.** The report gate (`lock_tx_reports(k)`) is taken by `reopen_tx_streamer` once and held across all attempts, as today it is across the one (`core.rs:1315-1318`).

## 2. Fakes and rigs

### 2.1 One new `FakeFault`: `DropAckOfSend(usize)`

*Shape.* "The burst ACKs (every channel position) of the `nth` `tx_send` carrying samples (0-based, the ordinal `ShortSend` uses) are not posted." *Where.* `device.rs:1812-1822` computes `nth` only inside the `cut` block; hoist it (one `let nth = …`) and pass `drop_ack = faults.contains(&DropBurstAck) || faults.contains(&DropAckOfSend(nth))` to `streamer.radio.packet(…)` at `device.rs:1865`. About six lines. *Why:* `DropBurstAck` is permanent, so "no ACK once, then ACK" cannot be written with it; `[DropAckOfSend(0), DropAckOfSend(1)]` drops attempts 1 and 2. *Faithfulness:* an ACK-less whole send is the hardware case `options.md` §2.1 item 2 describes (a dropped error-FIFO entry or lost report); the fake's DUC and radio state still advance, as on hardware.

Not needed: a stale or foreign ACK (`ReportFeed.give(BurstAck …, tick)` delivers to whichever streamer reads next); a zero, short or failed send (`StalledSend`, `ShortSend`, `FailSend`, one entry per attempt); a partial ACK (`[DropAckOfSend(0), TxReport(Wall::ZERO, BurstAck)]` on two channels); a failing time read (`TimeReadFailsFor`, `Lost` + `slow_next_read`).

### 2.2 Rigs

- **Core level** (`core.rs` tests): `rig_with(FakeConfig { clock: Manual, faults })` and the unregistered test thread, as `ur_22_t3_missing_ack_is_bounded_…` (`core.rs:1729`) does; `LockSpy` (`rig_on` + `spy.watch`) where the order of device calls is the observation; `ReportFeed` where a scripted report is; `test_support::probe(&core, name, closure)` where a gate must sit **outside** `device_gate` (every between-attempt gate, M4). - **Two clocks, never mixed (N1).** *Device time* is `device.time_now()` (the fake's clock: the host clock since `zero`, `device.rs:1073-1087`; on the manual host it advances through `host.sleep`, which the fake's `tx_async` wait and the loop's back-offs call; `released(frozen, host, || tx.pass())` lets that sleep run). ### 2.3 Expected call shapes (device calls; `time_now` is not a recorded call)

`reopen_tx_streamer` starts with the old streamer's close; `open_tx_confirmed_locked` called directly does not (drop the leading `close`).

| Outcome | Calls |
|---|---|
| confirmed on attempt 1 / 2 / 3 | `close, open, send` / `close, open, send, close, open, send` / (three pairs) |
| three unconfirmed | `close, open, send, close, open, send, close, open, send, close` (the last streamer is closed: nothing is left open) |

| unconfirmed by a non-whole send | **no** `tx_async` between the attempt's `send` and the next `close` |

| a pre-send time read that fails for a few ms and then succeeds | the attempt is as a normal one (`open, send`, …) after the back-offs |

### 2.4 Time budget on the manual clock

A whole send with no usable ACK costs exactly 100 ms of host time (`until`, `core.rs:1229`; the fake's `tx_async` sleeps its `remaining`, `device.rs:1983-1998`); a non-whole send costs 0; a pre-send time read that fails until the deadline costs 100 ms with no send, and one that fails for `m` ms and then succeeds costs `m` ms (1 ms back-offs, N3). Opens, closes and a successful time read cost 0 on the fake. So `elapsed` (device time, N1) is `0`, `100`, `200` ms for confirmed on 1/2/3 and `300` ms for three whole-send failures; assert `>= n × 100 ms` and `< n × 100 ms + 5 ms` (the 1 ms sleeps of `core.rs:1271` and `:1285` are absent for the faults used, except where C5e names the back-offs). F6's KL text quotes `3 × (open + 100 ms)`; the fake's open is 0 and the hardware's is unmeasured (INFERRED).
