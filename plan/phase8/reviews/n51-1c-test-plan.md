## M1 — W13's rewrite keeps the ordering P473 tests (§1 W13, §0.5, §3)

Sol: a blocking `recv` of the held owner freezes the clock (the owner is a busy participant), so a driver that polls on the clock cannot see the entry until the owner's 10 s timeout releases it: the test then passes without the Stop coming during the configuration. `until_received` is therefore **not added** (its only user was W13; no leftover). Instead `HoldAfterApply` becomes a three-flag object and **its hold is a clock wait**, so the clock runs while the owner is held and the driver's polls work:

- `hold(chan)` arms; the owner, in `apply`, sets `entered`, then waits for `released` by `host.sleep(1 ms)` polls (a participant idle on the clock) bounded by **10 s of real time**; if that bound ends the hold it sets `timed_out`.
- The test: Stop and `start_rx` pushed; `direct.until("the owner's configuration", |_| spy.entered())` (the horizon and the marker are `until`'s); **an explicit assertion that the owner is still held** (`entered && !released`) immediately before `Provider::stop`, and again after it: the Stop came *during* the configuration; after `release()`, if the owner's own bound had expired (`timed_out`), the test writes the infra marker and panics (the setup, not the code under test, ended the hold).
- P473's kill path: the mutant re-reads `Provider::stop` after the configuration and returns early, so `rx_start 1` is issued once, not twice: the existing `assert_eq!(calls(…"rx_start 1").len(), 2)` fails (an assertion, not a bound). Re-run in A.
- Not run with a hold that is not a clock wait: `until`'s horizon (5 s of device time) would end at ~5 s of real time, before the owner's 10 s.

## M3 — one rig-level rule for every wait that returns control to the test, on `Direct` and on `UhdRig`

A fixed epsilon is not a rule: `settle(999 µs)` from an armed rig ends at S + 1 ms, exactly uhd-control's next poll, and the wake of the driver and the poll tie (M4 holds the instant until both ran, in an unspecified order). A general rule needs two parts, both in the rigs, none in the Provider:

- **R1 (flush).** Every rig wait ends with a clock sleep of 1 µs after the instant it ended on (`end_wait`: `host.sleep(1 µs)`), so every participant due at *that* instant has run before the wait returns (M4: the clock stands at the instant until each due participant ran). `UhdRig::settle`, `wait_until` and the polls of `wait_for_events` use it; `Direct::wait`, `wait_until`, `settle`, `until`, `start` in B. For the observations a test makes after a wait (events, device calls).
- **R2 (receive side).** The rig's Action queue (`UhdRig`'s `Queue`, `Direct`'s `Pushed`) stamps each Action with the device clock at its push and `recv` withholds an Action until the clock is **strictly later** than its stamp (`Stamped<T>` in `tests/common/rig.rs`, one copy for both). An Action is therefore received at the first poll strictly after the instant it was pushed, whichever order the driver and the poll woke in; the 1 µs landing on a poll (`settle(999 µs)`) cannot split a receipt. Pushes made while the thread is a participant stamp an exact instant (the clock stands); the Actions the Kernel dispatches in other tests never pass through these queues. R2 is the "equivalent ordering" the decision allows, established where the order is decided (the receive) rather than at the final wake, because the final wake's neighbours (any participant's deadline at E + 1 µs) are not knowable from the rig.
- What R1+R2 do **not** promise (documented in the README): a participant due at E + 1 µs *itself* (not a poll) may or may not have run when the wait returns; a test asserts an event or call by its own device time or waits for it with `until`/`wait_for_events`, never by reading right after a wait. Supported phases are therefore all of them for Action receipts and every instant ≤ E for observations.
- **1b's tests.** `uhd_rig_settle_advances_…` and `uhd_rig_two_rigs_on_one_thread_…` assert that `UhdRig::settle(7 ms)` moves the clock by exactly 7 ms. Under the one rule `settle` gets R1's microsecond too, so these **two assertions change to 7 ms + 1 µs** (`Direct`'s T2 already expects it): the exact-duration claim they pinned is the rule M3 replaces, and 1b's own `wait_until` assertion changed the same way. Every other 1b assertion is unedited. 1b's rows N51B-06, -08 and -10 keep their anchors (the poll and the microsecond stay one statement each, the microsecond is in one method `end_wait`).
- **Tests** (names `…rig_…`): `rig_an_entry_is_taken_only_at_a_later_clock_instant_than_its_push` (deterministic, in `rig.rs`: a driver on a manual clock, push, `take` at the same instant is `None`, after a 1 µs clock sleep `Some`, FIFO); `uhd_rig_an_action_is_not_received_at_the_instant_it_was_pushed` (deterministic: `rig.push`, `rig.actions.recv()` is `None`; `settle(1 µs)`, `Some`); `uhd_rig_a_wait_that_ends_on_a_poll_instant_still_receives_at_the_next_poll` (the boundary case: from a started rig `settle` ends exactly on each of 12 grid instants, push, receipt at the next grid instant ±1 tick; a race pin, the two above are the deterministic killers); `Direct`'s three equivalents in B.
