## Contract and test method

The drain obligation is every timed DDC frequency entry handed to this receive chain that has not yet been shown consumed, whatever its instant relative to the logical cut. Consumption is shown by a received sample at or after the entry's instant, observed after the device accepted the handover; equal-time entries on one channel require one sample per entry. The no-delay path is valid only when every handed entry is already shown consumed. A logical cut remains unchanged: publish nothing at or after it.

Register an obligation before `apply`, under a per-stream lock also taken by the RX stop decision. It remains until consumption is shown, even after its logical row folds; remove it when `apply` fails or is refused. Gain, untimed/deferred frequency, and refused apply create no DDC obligation. Keep receiving and discard samples while draining. Device loss ends the wait immediately.

The hard deadline remains the later of (a) the latest outstanding entry instant plus one device RX packet duration at that stream's rate plus `DELIVERY_ALLOWANCE_NS`, and (b) the existing quiet-span/minimum receive-timeout path. Split that bound at the packet-duration boundary: continue the ordinary drain until `max(latest e + one receive-call duration, cut + one block duration)`; if entries remain, stop once, restart once, and use only the remaining time to the hard deadline for the flush. This lets the one flush fit inside the shared post-cut ready floor rather than adding a second receive-call duration. It must not cause an early `finish`, pruning, channel release, or successor configuration.

**RM-25/VH-4 timeline rule (owner decision §6.7):** after a receive cut, the next receive segment's ready floor is `cut + that stream's release window + one receive call at the old rate + delivery allowance`; its configured RX start lead is added after that ready floor. Apply this conservatively to every receive successor after a cut, including when no DDC value is outstanding; planning cannot depend on runtime commands. The first receive segment and transmit successors are unchanged. MockRadio and UHD use the same `timeline::State::begin` term, each populated from its own profile/description. The drain stop may therefore occur within (never consume) the successor's planned lead.

All Module-path tests use the manual device clock and bounded gates, not a wall-clock timing oracle. Observe RX calls/order, delivered blocks, FakeDevice `dsp_pending`/`dsp_log`/`freq_at`, applied records, timing/rejected rows, and events. A host watchdog may catch a hang but is not a timing assertion. A test-specific controlled Device adapter or an explicit FakeFault models actual DDC flow interruption; host-delivery silence is not flow interruption.

Evidence: UHD 4.10.0.0 `axi_tag_time.v:91–97,207–211` gates timed FIFO consumption on a valid timestamped sample transfer; `ddc.v:114–144` has a separate untimed register path. X300 has no timed RX STOP (`radio_rx_core.v:171–183`). Bench P4 A/C showed that an unconsumed DDC setting survives stop and streamer close/reopen, then affects the next stream's first sample on UBX and OBX; P4 B AUTO/AUTO left none (`plan/phase8/bench-results.md`, 2026-10-29 D9 probe, §P4). FakeDevice's model observations are `device.rs:517–536,1108–1129`, not RF-tone verdicts.
