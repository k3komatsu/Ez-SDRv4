## 1. Model the tests assume (so each assertion can be checked against it)

### 1.1 FakeDevice (sub-step A)

- **State.** `Radio` gains: `late: bool` (the current device burst's head was dropped late; cleared by an SOB and by the burst's EOB), and a drain record (`drain_start`, `drained` = S samples so far, both in ticks/samples, reset by `reset_clock`, `device.rs:693-697`, and by a drop that starts after the previous drain's limit).
- **What drains.** Only packets dropped on account of lateness: the idle-late check (`:758-765`) and every later packet of a burst whose head it dropped (`late` set; these go through `Burst::Timed`/`Silent`, `faulted`, `:812-838`, whatever their own timing), and an SOB dropped because it falls before the drain's limit (below). The guard refusal (`:750-757`), the undersized timed packet (`:733-738`) and the armed-burst rest keep their UR-33 rules unchanged and do **not** drain (Q12: UR-33 says a refused SOB's EOB arms no guard, `device.rs:3371-3380`, and those bursts are not late drops).
- **Arithmetic.** A dropped packet of `n` samples at `ratio` starts at `from = max(now, cursor, drain_raw_end)`, where `drain_raw_end = drain_start + S*ratio` is the end of what the drain has taken so far; its TimeError (where it gets one) is one per channel with `tick = from`; then `S += n`. The drain's **limit** is `drain_start + (S + pad + g) * ratio` with `pad = max(g, ceil(S/100))` samples and `g = short_gap(ratio)` (`device.rs:711-714`: 15 at 1 MS/s). It is the addendum's rule (drain ends at its samples plus the pad, then the guard `g` from there), kept in the drain record rather than in `short_until`, so the UR-33 guard state and its tests are untouched (Q12). The pad and `g` do not move report ticks (they follow real-time consumption).
- **Idle check.** An SOB handed with `t < drain limit` while the radio is idle is late: one TimeError per channel, tick `from`, the burst dropped through its EOB (`late` set, so it drains too and S grows). `t >= limit` is untouched.
- A late-idle drop of a packet that is not the burst's EOB sets `burst = Burst::Timed`: later timed packets one TimeError per channel each, through the EOB, no BurstAck; the first later untimed packet one TimeError, then silence (UR-33's existing rule; the X310 plays that untimed tail late, H1 second cell, so the fake is stricter there and UR-33 says so).
- The TimeError tick is **not** held back until the fake's clock reaches it (BurstAck is). A tick can be ahead of `now`; it keeps every existing `tx_async` test free of new waits, and a report that arrives early only makes the Module abandon earlier than hardware would (Q2).
- **No claim about the cascade boundary.** With the pad the fake is stricter than the device after a drain (the device's drain is later by two clocks per DUC packet and the DUC's between-burst latency, about 0.1 % at 1 996-sample packets, more at small first packets; the pad is 1 % of S, at least `g`). The pad is the fake's own margin and is INFERRED to cover the device (bench cell C4 measures it). No test or row pins a device value for it. **Test placements:** a burst *clearly inside* the drain is at least 1 000 samples (at 1 MS/s) before the drain's raw end; *clearly past* is at least 1 000 samples after the limit; the **band tests F3b, F3b2 and F3c** place a burst inside the pad, the guard or the floor part of the limit, between the two limits the probe distinguishes with at least 6 samples on each side, and are named and commented as the fake's own margin, never as device behaviour. UR-33 states this (§7). No flow control after a drain.

### 3.4 Hardware-facing: one lifted body with a rehearsal (sub-step C, Q4; recommended small, else deferred to the bench-tools brief)

- `tests/common/mt3.rs` (new, pattern of `common/e3.rs`): `late_chunked_burst(device, rate, chunk, late_ms)` runs one trial of the Module's shape on the raw device: RX started first on the loopback; a **control burst** (the same chunked shape, on time, a known tone) sent and heard; then the late burst (SOB on the first chunk, every chunk timed with its first sample's time, EOB on the last, the host `late_ms` late), with its expected interval `[first_tick, first_tick + n*ratio)` known. It returns the RX blocks as `(first_tick, len)` with the call's errors and `other` entries (the shape of `read_until_tick_without_clock`, `hardware.rs:1609-1621`), the heard intervals (`observations::heard_intervals`, `observations/mod.rs:14`), the control's heard interval, the reports and the ack presence. On `FakeDevice` the receive path loops back when the TX and RX frequencies are equal, as in `raw_rx`/`raw_tx`.
- A **pure verdict** `late_chunked_verdict(..)`, in this order (M1): (1) **FAIL** if a BurstAck for the late burst is present, or a heard interval lies inside the late burst's expected interval (partial transmission is positive evidence: it holds whatever the coverage and the control); (2) **INCONCLUSIVE** if `rx_start` failed, if the RX blocks do not cover the late burst's interval plus a margin of one chunk on each side without a gap (a gap is a block starting after the previous one's end, or a missing block at either edge), if any `other` entry (overflow, timeout, error) lies in the interval, or if the control burst was not heard with its expected amplitude and delay; (3) **INCONCLUSIVE** if the coverage and control are fine but there is no TimeError (the burst was not late: nothing was proved); (4) **PASS** only with complete coverage, a heard control, nothing heard in the late burst's interval, no BurstAck and at least one TimeError. Missing or incomplete RX evidence is never PASS. A cell passes only if every trial passes; one FAIL fails it; otherwise INCONCLUSIVE.
- **Synthetic verdict cases** (unit tests in `common/mt3.rs`, rows built in the test, shaped like H1's trials): complete coverage + heard control + nothing heard + TimeErrors + no ack: PASS; **absent RX** (no blocks, or `rx_start` error) with everything else as the PASS case: INCONCLUSIVE, not PASS; **incomplete RX** (a gap inside the interval; blocks ending before the interval's end; the edge margin missing): INCONCLUSIVE; control not heard: INCONCLUSIVE; an `other` entry inside the interval: INCONCLUSIVE; **observed partial transmission** (a heard interval inside the late burst's interval) with complete coverage: FAIL, and the same with incomplete coverage: still FAIL; a BurstAck: FAIL; no TimeError with complete coverage: INCONCLUSIVE. ## 9. Repeat resume (sub-step D): a repeat resumes after a late drop or an underflow

Sources: `addendum.md` (design), `fable-addendum-review.md` (M1–M5, n1–n3), `decisions.md` from "REPEAT RESUME" on (every entry binds; where the addendum and a later decision differ, the decision wins: split records, optional `resumes_at`, one `timing` row, the b′ stutter rule, the switch rule, the missed-R rule). Read, not edited: the worktree at `af1324c` (sub-steps A and B), `tx.rs` and `staging.rs` as they stand there. Nothing is designed here; the plan only fixes what each decision must show in a test.

### 9.0 Notation and rig numbers

1 MS/s: a sample is 200 root ticks; the switch rig's window is 10 ms, device lead 2 ms, a send is at most 2 500 samples (W/4), `g = 15`; `m = 10 ms` = 10 000 samples (its own constant, not `tx_in_flight_ns`). `H` is the handed cursor at the drop (the first sample the device has nothing past, `TxBook::cursor`, kept as its own fact while resuming, M3). `busy` is the Module's own bound on when the device finishes all it was handed: after each send the device took whole, `busy = max(busy, r, x) + n` (`r` = `core.now()` when the send returned, `x` its first-sample instant, none for the untimed T3, `n` its samples, padding included). `R` is the resume point `repeat_boundary(origin, N, max(busy + m, now + lead, front))`, taken after the drop's close returns; `origin, N` the open booking's lattice (a switch's `b` starts its own). `P`, the stall part, is `(busy + m) - H` at the resume (the wait from there to the period boundary is not counted). `resumes_at` is the optional payload field (a plan); a part's real start is the next record's `target`. A **part** is the device burst sent at R. Examples quote samples at 1 MS/s; the expected `R` of every test is worked out by hand in the test's comment from the `r`, `x`, `n` it scripts, never by calling the production function (R14 tests the function itself).

Rig honesty (the one trap): in the unit rigs the Module's clock (`core`) and the device's are separate and `device_at` moves both; a late first send is made by moving only `device.set_time` (the pattern of `late_burst`, `mt3.rs:171`). That leaves `r` at the Module's old time, so for a resume test the owner's clock is brought to the device's with `device_at` **after the late send and before the reports are read** (the stall is noticed), or the stall is put inside the send with `test_support::probe(core, "tx_send_planned", ..)` (V4). A test that leaves the owner behind the device gets an `R` inside the drain and tests the zero case by accident.

## 10. Open questions, with my recommendation

- **Q1 Tickless TimeError.** Recommend: raw only (U15), UR-28's "first-pending" sentence stays for `BurstAck` only. A tickless TimeError naming the first pending would name one burst per report in a flood. Hardware always ticks (E6) and the fake does, so only scripted tests reach it. Internal (orchestrator).
- **Q2 Dropped packets and flow control, held TimeError ticks.** Recommend: neither. Flow control of dropped packets is INFERRED for UHD and risks blocking manual-clock tests whose chunk is W/4; a held tick would make every drop test wait for the clock. The cost: the fake's reports can be ahead of its clock, said in UR-33. Internal.
- **Q3 Boundary of the drain (revised in v2).** With the pad the fake is stricter than the device at the cascade boundary, so a fake test may pin the fake's own band as long as it never states a device value: F3b, F3b2 and F3c each probe between the two limits they distinguish with at least 6 samples on each side (pad term, guard, floor, one witness each); no test or row is sample-exact (`<` to `<=` at the limit survives and no row is added for it).
- **Q12 (new, internal) Where the pad and guard live.** The addendum says the drain "arms the existing guard `g` from there". Implemented literally, a late drop would set `short_until`, which contradicts UR-33's "a refused SOB's EOB arms no guard" (`device.rs:3371-3380`, `N49B-08`) and changes the guard tests' meaning. Recommend a limit of its own in the drain record (§1.1): the same observable rule (an SOB before `drain end + pad + g` is refused) with the guard state and its tests untouched, and only late drops (the idle-late check, the rest of a late-headed burst, an SOB before the limit) drain; guard refusals and undersized packets keep their UR-33 rules. Decided here as my recommendation; the orchestrator may reverse it, which would move F3b, F3b2 and F3c's expectations and add the guard tests to the audit.
- **Q4 Does this step include the lifted chunked cell (§3.4)?** Recommend yes, small: without it the fake's new claim has no rehearsal with a hardware twin, which AGENTS §9 asks for when an `#[ignore]` test's expectation comes from a rule, and C1 is the first bench cell O7 wants. If the orchestrator prefers, defer it to the bench-tools brief; nothing else depends on it.
- **Q5 Does a joined repeat-switch booking count as a Kernel burst?** Yes, same rule (its own booking target); its test is §9.3 RT20 (V21).
- **Q6 Are joiners appended in the failed/short-send branches?** Recommend yes, mirroring `remember_burst` (U4), so the event matches what may have gone.
- **Q7 Abandon predicate.** Recommend the same `underflowed(tick)` (open and `tick >= first`) rather than "the retired entry is the open burst": one predicate, no new identity between the entry and the chain. Consequence: an open burst with no entry (cleared by a generation change) is still abandoned by a TimeError that reaches it; acceptable and covered by U10's stale case.
- **Q9 T3 behind a drain (Fable R10/N3).** In the plan's model an untimed T3 flush plays at `max(cursor, now)` at once even inside a drain; the X310 queues it behind the drain (and may play the dropped burst's last tens of samples untimed, R2). Recommend: **declare, do not model**: UR-33 lists it among the looser cases (with the cascade boundary), KL-45 states it, and U13 does not depend on it: the reopen replaces the streamer (`TxStreamer::new`, a fresh `Radio` with no drain) and its T3 confirmation is judged on the new queue, so the test passes on the fake whichever way T3 behaves in a drain. Modelling it would need the fake to block an untimed packet on the drain limit and the T3's 100 ms deadline interplay (#75), which is new mechanism for a case a reopen already avoids.
- **Q10 (resolved in v4)** The interim in sub-step B (a late repeat reported and retired but not abandoned) was the orchestrator's decision (b); sub-step D removes it (§9.1, RT1, RT21) and B lands only together with D.
- **Q11 My reading of m1.** The brief says "one channel's report alone retires the burst". I read that as: the first channel report that reaches an entry makes its events and counts the burst as dropped (the entry keeps its other channels until each is retired by its own report, an ACK or a later TimeError, silently once `told`). The alternative (retire every channel of the entry on the first report) would lose a second channel's ACK matching. Recommend my reading; U9 pins both channel orders.
- **Q8 A TimeError that reaches nothing and a burst that was joined after the entry's retirement.** Not decided here: a Kernel burst joined into a device burst after a report already retired its entry cannot exist (the abandon closes the chain on the same turn), so no test; if the resume design keeps a device burst open across a late report this needs a test (§9).
