**A.2 Sol's findings.**

- **M1 (the clock-waiting operations).** Recovery, reopen, the T3 flush and shutdown wait on the device's host clock (`provider/core.rs:1209-1234`), which `Frozen` stops (`provider/test_support.rs:136-157`). Every such operation in C1, C3 and C4, and in every mutation killer built on them, runs through the existing `released` participant helper (`test_support.rs:170-184`): the owner and a new freeze come back before the snapshot is read; the freeze is never dropped around an unregistered test thread. Implemented: `pass_released` (C3, C3b), `released` (C1's flush, C4's reopen, C3's shutdown). §3.3's "every new test is single-threaded on a frozen or stepped rig" now reads: each new test runs on its own thread with the clock frozen, except the operations above, which run as participants of the host clock under `released`; no verdict depends on when a thread runs. Because a participant thread is involved, those tests (C1, C3, C3b, C4) were run 20 times and 120 more under load (6 parallel loops of 20): no difference.
- **m1 (counts cannot see a write moved between two sections).** C3/C4 counts and operation-exit snapshots cannot tell a write moved from one section to the next. C3b (`e3b_c3b_the_seams_between_the_sections`) adds ordered snapshots at the three seams: (1) after `complete_send` and before the chain write, reached as the owner's third `rec` lock of a send (`stat("tx_bursts")` for a first send, `stat("tx_samples")` for a continuation), with `at_nth_lock`; (2) `end_open`'s two streams sections, the first before the chain is closed and the device called, the second after the close's result; (3) the flush's front against the reopen's result section, reached by arming `on_next_lock` from the `tx_reopen_done` probe. At each seam the hook records what the book, the device and the record show and the test asserts the seam fired once and that the receipt (the record's runs over the booked region) is the same before, between and after: receipt still reads the prediction. **The chain itself cannot be read from a hook while it is thread-private to `Tx`**: the seam lines show the book (open booking's next sample and first flag), the device's sends and the record, and the chain is shown at the operation's exit (`chain:` lines). E3b-3 puts the chain in the book, so its hooks read it; the seam lines then gain a `chain` column. **Permitted differences at E3b-4** (the fold moves the chain write into `complete`, `abandon`, `end_open`, `close_unjoined` and `complete_reopen`): at seam (1) the chain column is the *old* state before the fold (the chain is written after `complete_send`) and the *new* state after it; at seam (2) section 1 shows the old chain (open, tail kept) and section 2 the closed chain with its front advanced both before and after the fold, since the close's own section is the one that returns the result; at seam (3) the flush front is applied before the result section before the fold and inside it after. Every other column of every seam line, and every receipt, must be equal before and after.
- **m2 (the join exemption).** P-MH's "first send fits implies may have handed" needs "no preceding open join" (`segment.rs:135-147` `handed_end` ignores preceding chains; `staging.rs:1103-1107` `may_be_handed` suppresses a chain behind an open join). Pinned separately: `e3b_p_mh_the_join_exemption_is_not_in_handed_end` (C of 40 at 70 fits a window of 200, `handed_end` is `Some(110)`; A of 40 at 0 can still be joined; the booking at 45 is accepted at the join deadline J(40) = 140 and refused from 141). - **m3 (checked against saturating).** The receipt's front uses checked addition (`staging.rs:1128-1133`: a front that does not fit an `i64` makes `accept` return `Err(Booking)`), the executor's saturating (`tx.rs:826, 866, 942, 1026`). The shared functions' contract: the receipt prediction (`predicted_front`) is `Option<i64>`, `None` for an overflow, and `accept` turns `None` into a refusal, never a saturated accepted footprint; the executor's `close_front` is `i64` and saturates. Boundary rows for every close shape: the receipt side runs today (`e3b_p_cf_the_receipt_front_near_the_end_of_the_range`: short head 1, 4 and 32, long head 33 and 100, a joined piece of 10 and a joined one-sample piece, each at its largest fitting start and the next; a repeat). The executor side is not reachable through a rig: a send at samples near `i64::MAX` fails in the manual clock first (`ManualTimeAuthority::advance_to` returns `Overflow` at 4 000 samples below the range's end, probed), so its saturating rows (the direct close's `next + pad + gap`, the Stop's and end's `close_start + 2 + CLOSE_GAP`, the abandon's `cursor + 2 + g`, **the flush's `at_or_after(tick - 1) + g`**) are pure rows of `segment::close_front` and of the flush's call in E3b-2, written with the function: each at `i64::MAX - gap - k`, expected `i64::MAX`, and `predicted_front` expected `None` at the same inputs. P-G2 (E3b-2) covers both ends.
- **K5 (the search of every `Tx::new` caller, complete).** VERIFIED by `grep -rn "Tx::new(" --include=*.rs crates`: one production caller, `provider/mod.rs:662`, in `start` once per transmit stream (`for stream in &tx_streams`); `start` is called by the Kernel once per Run (INFERRED; `start` itself does not guard a second call, `mod.rs:579`). ## 0. Summary, decisions for the orchestrator, and where the records contradict the code

**What E3b is, read from the brief.** Move `TxChain` (`tx.rs:94-219`: the device burst, its next sample, the held tail, the safe front, recovery) into the book (`TxBook`), so one state machine owns it; replace the record's front prediction (`staging.rs:1128` `front()`, the `Chain.front` field it fills) and `may_be_handed` (`staging.rs:1106`) by answers computed through `segment.rs`, so the front has one arithmetic; the stat `tx_close_past_record_front` is **kept** (K2, decided). **Intended: no behaviour change.**

**The finding that decides the plan.** The record's front is a *prediction* made at receipt, before any send exists; the executor's front is the *actual* one after a close. They are equal only for two close shapes (a short head's padded close, a long head's held-tail close). In six sequences they differ today, each with an observable receipt-versus-dispatch difference (§1.4, F1–F6; F1 and F4 are new, probed and not pinned jointly or at all; F5's margin is new and probed). So "answers computed from that state" read literally (receipt reads the executor's actual state) **is a behaviour change** in five of them, and one of them is user-visible text (KL-37). The plan therefore rests on the reading that keeps every receipt answer and still gives one arithmetic:

**Shape S (assumed by every test below; the orchestrator decides, §7).**
1. `segment.rs` gets the one front arithmetic: a close send's exclusive end plus a gap, `close_front(send_start, samples_sent, gap) = send_start + samples_sent + gap` (UR-22's own sentence). Every front in the tree is an instance of it: a short head's padded close `(start, max(len,4), g)`; a held-tail close `(end - 1, 2, CLOSE_GAP)` = `HELD_TAIL_FRONT`; a Stop's or an end's two-zero close `(cursor, 2, CLOSE_GAP)` = `STOP_FRONT`; an abandon's `(cursor, 2, g)`; the flush `(at_or_after(tick - 1), 0, g)`; the direct close `(next, device_count, gap)` that `send` writes today (`tx.rs:821-826`). The record's booking-time front becomes `segment::predicted_front(...)`: the **envelope** of the executor's possible shapes for that booking (it takes the held-tail shape for any head over 32 samples and any joined piece, because `hold` depends on a `now` nobody has at receipt). `staging::front()`, `CLOSE_GAP`, `HELD_TAIL_FRONT`, `STOP_FRONT` become derived from it; the closing sends in `tx.rs` call it.
2. `may_be_handed` becomes a pure `segment::may_have_handed(start, horizon, ...)` with the same answers (the window rule of RM-15 and KL-37), beside `handed_end` (MR-25's window-only mode, E3a). The two are different questions (§1.4 F5) and stay two functions; they share the constants.
3. `DeviceChain<T>` (burst, front, recovery) is a field of `TxBook<P, T = ()>` in `staging.rs`; `T` is the held tail's payload (`Tail` on UHD; the Mock never has one). The owner writes it only through book transitions; the receipt side still reads **only the record**, as `core.rs:399` says ("never the hand-over state").
4. The stat stays (K2). The Mock does not drive the chain (K3).

**Decisions for the orchestrator** (recommendation first; each is in the E3a style, K = decision):
