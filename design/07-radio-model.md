## 1. Purpose

Vision §5 puts "channels, streams, RF parameters, TimingEnvelope, PerformanceEnvelope, coherence basis, repeat / decimation capabilities" in the Radio Model, a Vocabulary: versioned on its own, additive, and never read by the Kernel by name (OV-21). This document is that Vocabulary's first version. Every radio Provider — MockRadio (spec 09) and the UHD Provider (spec 18) — implements the same keys with the same meaning, which is what makes Vision §59's "change only the BindingProfile" testable.

## 2. Evidence

- **UHD coerces rate, gain and frequency** to a device grid and documents reading them back (audit Appendix B; spec 03 §2). Hence RM-8's coercion rules and the per-key coercion defaults Vision §11 names: `reject` for sample rate and frequency, `warn` for gain.
- **UHD's overflow**: zero samples returned, `out_of_sequence` distinguishing a host-side sequence error, a continuous stream restarting after `OVERRUN_RESTART_DELAY` = 0.05 s (audit Finding 3, VERIFIED). Hence RM-17 and RM-18.
- **UHD resets the CORDICs on every start of burst and needs a time spec on each** (spec 02 §2). Hence RM-13's one waveform per burst and the Kernel's `BurstTracker` (SC-29).
- **A late timed command** on UHD yields `ERROR_CODE_LATE_COMMAND` on receive and `EVENT_CODE_TIME_ERROR` on transmit (audit §10's UHD row). Spec 04 §9 issue 2 separates the two: `TIME_ERROR` for a burst whose target has passed, `LATE_COMMAND` for a control command issued too late. Hence RM-10.
- **Retune phase** on a UBX or an OBX is random unless the retune is timed (audit Finding 15, VERIFIED against UHD's `page_sync`), and a CBX has no timed LO phase synchronisation at all (`sync_phase` is the UBX's and the OBX's; design 33 D13), so its phase is random on every retune. Hence RM-9's three values.

## 3. Model

```text
Provider instance tree (one per radio instance)
└── <device>                kind radio.device       the node a Spec binds (Y5)
    │   ports:  rx<k>  out  ezsdr.stream.cf32       one per receive channel
    │   capabilities: per-channel, per-stream and instance-wide values
    ├── <device>/rx/s<k>     kind radio.rx_stream    stream id: SampleClock owner, event source
    └── <device>/tx/s<k>     kind radio.tx_stream    stream id: SampleClock owner, TxBurst target

configuration (PrepareReport.effective; per fragment, SB-30)
  radio.{rx,tx}.c<n>.{frequency_hz, gain_db, antenna, stream}
  radio.{rx,tx}.s<k>.sample_rate_hz

instance-wide capabilities
  radio.full_duplex   radio.hardware_time   radio.tx.repeat_*
  radio.rx.block_len   radio.timing.*   radio.perf.*
```

## 4. Rules

### Identity and registration

- **RM-1** The Vocabulary is `VocabularyDescriptor { id: radio, version: 2.0.0, prefix: radio, keys: RM-4's and RM-27's tables, event_kinds: RM-10's table, verbs: RM-12's table, checks: [radio.rf_envelope] }`, returned by `ezsdr_radio::vocabulary()`. `ezsdr_radio::register(registry, checks, kinds)` registers the descriptor, the `RfEnvelopeCheck` of RM-19 and every event kind with owner `Some(radio)`, in that order, and is the only way a runtime assembles the Vocabulary.
  *Test:* `rm_01_register_adds_the_descriptor_the_check_and_the_kinds`.

### Resource tree

- **RM-2** A radio Provider instance exposes one `radio.device` root with `<device>/rx/s<k>` and `<device>/tx/s<k>` children of kind `radio.rx_stream` and `radio.tx_stream`; stream children have no capabilities, ports or memory domains. The root declares one `rx<k>` output port of contract `ezsdr.stream.cf32` per receive channel and its host memory domain. The port name `tx` (direction `in`) is reserved for link-fed transmission and is not declared. A Spec resource that uses a radio is of kind `radio.device`. No node of a radio tree is `shareable`.
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `mr_02_the_tree_has_the_radio_model_shape`, `rm_29_the_layout_description_builds_the_stream_tree` and `d3_the_layout_is_a_node_id_of_neither_provider`.
- **RM-3** A stream SampleClock is declared on `<device>/rx/s<k>` or `<device>/tx/s<k>` (TM-13a). Its block's `channels` is the size of its channel set, and block channel `i` is that set's `i`-th smallest logical channel (RM-30, SC-10). A stream that holds no channel has no SampleClock or block; every `TxBurst` targeting it is refused (KC-24).
  *Producer obligation.*

### Envelopes

- **RM-6** The **TimingEnvelope** is the five `radio.timing.*` capabilities, each declared on the device node as `One(Int(v))`, in nanoseconds: the minimum timed-command lead, the startup latency, the overflow restart gap, the receive start lead and the transmit in-flight window. A radio Provider sets `ProviderInstance.min_command_lead` to `Some(Duration(host.monotonic, radio.timing.min_timed_command_lead_ns))` when that value is positive and to `None` when it is 0 (MA-10). A Spec expresses a timing requirement as a constraint on these keys; the generic matcher refuses a Provider that cannot meet it at `validate()` (Vision §13 rule 2). The `min_timed_command_lead_ns` is the end-to-end lead the Provider needs from the coordinator's dispatch, including its delivery allowance.
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `mr_03_profile_values_reach_the_capabilities_and_the_envelope_section`, `ur_09_profile_values_reach_the_capabilities_and_the_envelope_section` and `sb_06_constraint_match_table`; `ProviderInstance.min_command_lead` (`Some` for a positive lead, `None` for 0) by `mr_04_instance` and `ur_10_instance`.
- **RM-7** The **PerformanceEnvelope** is the three `radio.perf.*` capabilities. A layout configuration is admissible only when the sum, over streams in each direction, of channel count × stream rate × wire bytes per sample does not exceed that direction's throughput limit (RM-32). A radio Provider refuses an inadmissible configuration in `coerce`, and in `prepare`.
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `mr_06_coerce_cases`; the `prepare` refusal by `mr_07_prepare_cases`.
- **RM-8** **Coercion.** A numeric configuration key is coerced to its declared grid: the sample rates the device node declares as `AnyOf`, and each channel's frequency and gain to multiples of that channel's `radio.d.c<n>.frequency_step_hz` and `radio.d.c<n>.gain_step_db` within its declared ranges (RM-27). For an `Eq(v)` constraint the applied value is the grid value nearest `v`, the lower of two equally near; a `Coercion { key, requested: v, applied, reason }` is recorded exactly when `applied ≠ v` numerically (SB-6). A value outside the range is not coerced into it: the key is `rejected`. For `Min`, `Max`, `Range` and `Set` constraints the applied value is, respectively, the smallest grid value at or above the bound, the largest at or below, the smallest inside the range, and the first member of the set on the grid; none existing is a rejection, and none of these is a coercion. `Present` takes the default.
  A layout description coerces its channel and stream keys by the same rule (RM-30). On `x3xx` a transmit stream's rate grid is the device's grid from 1 MS/s up (decimation `N ≤ 200`, 200 rates) and a receive stream's is the whole device grid (639 rates): a transmit rate below 1 MS/s is outside the grid's range and is `rejected`, never coerced to 1 MS/s; `ideal` has one grid for both directions. The device still reports every rate (UR-13), so the device's whole grid is what it is compared with.
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `mr_06_coerce_cases` and `rm_30_coerce_cases`; the transmit grid by `rm_8_a_transmit_rate_below_one_msps_is_rejected_on_x3xx`, `mr_06_a_transmit_rate_below_one_msps_is_rejected_on_x3xx`, `ur_11_a_transmit_rate_below_one_msps_is_rejected`, `d3_the_two_providers_refuse_a_transmit_rate_below_one_msps_alike` and `x3xx_the_transmit_grid_is_the_ddc_s_up_to_decimation_200`; none named for the `Max` and `Range` applied values and for a `Min`/`Max`/`Range`/`Set` that finds no grid value on the receive grid.

### Coherence

  *Test:* `d3_a_cbx_layout_is_not_coherent_on_either_provider`; the `deterministic` value by `rm_29_the_ideal_layout_builds_its_tree_and_defaults` and `mr_34_the_deterministic_profile_keeps_every_phase_zero`.

### Events

- **RM-10** The event kinds are exactly these, registered with owner `radio` (RS-27):

  | Kind | Severity | Default reaction | Emitted when |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `radio.RX_OVERFLOW` | warning | mark_artifact | a receive overrun or sequence error lost samples (RM-17, RM-18) |
  | `radio.TX_UNDERFLOW` | warning | mark_artifact | a transmit stream ran out of samples inside a burst (SC-25) |
  | `radio.TX_DISCONTINUITY` | warning | mark_artifact | the burst tracker reported a discontinuity (SC-24a) |
  | `radio.TIME_ERROR` | error | mark_artifact | a burst's target was too close or past (RM-14), or the device model saw an unclosed burst followed by a timed block (spec 02 decision S12) |
  | `radio.LATE_COMMAND` | warning | mark_artifact | a stream start arrived with too little lead (MR-11) |
  | `radio.ALIGNMENT_ERROR` | error | mark_artifact | channels of one stream lost alignment (SC-13, SC-31) |
  | `radio.CLOCK_LOST` | fatal | abort | the device lost its reference |
  | `radio.START_LEAD_SHORT` | warning | continue | a receive start's configure ended with less than the declared command lead (RM-6) before the planned origin; the start keeps its origin (RM-25) |
  | `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED` | error | mark_artifact | the Provider could not carry out an admitted Action: an unsupported Action, a malformed waveform, a target it does not have, a configuration its envelope refuses |

  MockRadio never emits `radio.TX_UNDERFLOW`, `radio.TX_DISCONTINUITY`, `radio.ALIGNMENT_ERROR` or `radio.CLOCK_LOST`; the UHD Provider emits all but `radio.TX_DISCONTINUITY` (spec 18 UR-19, UR-27, UR-28).
  *Test:* `rm_10_the_kinds_are_registered_under_radio_with_their_defaults`; none named for MockRadio's four never-emitted kinds and for the UHD Provider's never emitting `radio.TX_DISCONTINUITY`.
- **RM-11** Every radio event is emitted with `emit_control`, except `radio.RX_OVERFLOW`, which every radio Provider must emit on the hot path (`EventSink::emit`, RS-32) with RM-24's bytes. The source node is the stream's own `<device>/rx/s<k>` or `<device>/tx/s<k>` node (RM-3) for `radio.RX_OVERFLOW`, `radio.ALIGNMENT_ERROR`, `radio.TX_UNDERFLOW` and `radio.TIME_ERROR`; the device node for `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED` and `radio.CLOCK_LOST`; and for `radio.LATE_COMMAND` the device node when it answers a start, and the receive stream's node when it reports that stream's missed timed start (spec 18 UR-17); and the receive stream's node for `radio.START_LEAD_SHORT`. No Provider has a transmit port fed by a link yet, so none emits `radio.TX_DISCONTINUITY`; SC-24a's Producer obligation is the first such Provider. `time` is in the stream's SampleClock or, before one exists, the Authority's primary root, and the payload is, on the control path, a JSON object with exactly these members:

  | Kind | Payload |
  |---|---|
  | `radio.RX_OVERFLOW` | `{ "cause": "overrun" \| "sequence", "lost": int }`; on the hot path, RM-24's bytes |
  | `radio.TIME_ERROR` | `{ "cause": "late" \| "unclosed_burst", "outcome": "send_asap" \| "drop" \| "plan_violation" \| "refused" \| "late_at_device", "late_by_ns": int, "target": TimePoint }` |
  | `radio.LATE_COMMAND` | `{ "key": string \| null, "requested": TimePoint, "applied": TimePoint }` |
  | `radio.START_LEAD_SHORT` | `{ "requested": TimePoint, "configured": TimePoint, "lead_left_ns": int, "min_lead_ns": int }`: `requested` the planned origin, `configured` the instant the start's configure ended, `lead_left_ns` the time between them, `min_lead_ns` the declared command lead (RM-6) it fell short of |
  | `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED` | `{ "action"?: ActionId, "kind": string, "reason": string }` (`kind` is the Action's `kind` tag; `action` is the id it was dispatched with (`Dispatched.id`, MA-14) when the Provider refuses it on receipt, and is absent when something it booked earlier is refused as it applies, when that id is no longer at hand) |
  | `radio.TX_UNDERFLOW` | `{ "cause": "starved" \| "lost" }`: `starved`, the device ran out of samples inside a burst because the host was late; `lost`, samples of a burst were lost between host and device |
  | `radio.ALIGNMENT_ERROR` | `{ "lost": int }`: the samples, on every channel of the stream, that the misalignment removed, which is the next block's time jump |
  | `radio.CLOCK_LOST` | `{ "reference": "frequency" }`: the frequency reference (10 MHz) was lost; no Provider monitors its time reference (PPS), so no other value is declared |

  `TIME_ERROR`'s outcome `late_at_device` means that the Provider handed the burst to the device in time by its own clock and the device reported that it arrived too late, so nothing was transmitted; `late_by_ns` is the device's reported instant less the target when the device reports one, and 0 otherwise.

  Each payload is the JSON serialisation of the RM-22 type of its kind, never a hand-built object.
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `rm_22_payloads_round_trip`, `mr_29_other_actions_are_command_rejected`, `mr_10_events_carry_the_source_the_context_gave`, `mr_16_burst_refusals`, `mr_37_the_overflow_travels_the_hot_path`, `mr_11_start_cases`, `ur_19_reports_before_one_block_keep_each_kind`, `mr_28_a_layout_instance_s_events_name_their_stream_node`, `ur_27_a_lost_reference_is_clock_lost` (the device node as `CLOCK_LOST`'s source) and `ur_28_a_layout_transmit_report_names_its_stream` (the stream node as `TX_UNDERFLOW`'s); none named for the receive stream's node as the source of `LATE_COMMAND` on a missed timed start.

### Verbs and bursts

- **RM-12** The Session verbs are: `start_repeat` → `CompileRule::TxBurst { repeat: true, late_policy: send_asap_and_flag }`, whose params are the burst's metadata: empty, or the switch mark that makes it a repeat switch (RM-13, RM-37); `send` → `CompileRule::TxBurst { repeat: false, late_policy: drop_and_flag }`. Their target is the transmit stream node `<resource>/tx/s<k>` (RM-13). `start_rx` → `CompileRule::Command {}`: its target is the receive stream node `<resource>/rx/s<k>`, it carries no params, and it turns the stream on again after a `Stop` as RM-21 says; its `at` is the instant at or after which it takes effect, never late. A stream's node may also be the target of a `Stop` (RM-13, RM-16): `ezsdr_radio::stream_target` reads `"<device>/rx/s<k>"` and `"<device>/tx/s<k>"` — `k` in SB-2a's spelling, without a leading zero — and no other id.
  *Test:* `rm_12_the_verbs_compile_as_declared` (the verb names, the `CompileRule`s and the registration); `ea_16_a_marked_start_repeat_switches_and_an_unmarked_one_is_refused` (the params reach the Provider as the burst's metadata: the mark switches, none is refused on a playing stream); `mr_29_start_rx_is_the_one_command` (the target, the empty params and the restart after a `Stop`); `mr_29_start_rx_takes_effect_at_its_at`, `mr_29_a_receive_link_never_begins_before_t0` and `mr_29_start_rx_is_never_late` (the `at`, the T0 floor a receive link keeps, and the never-late instant); `rm_12_a_stream_node_names_its_direction_and_number` (which ids name a stream).
- **RM-13** A `TxBurst` targets `<device>/tx/s<k>` and its `ArtifactRef` holds complex samples in that stream's `ezsdr.stream.cf32` contract, channel-interleaved (sample `n` of channel `c` at byte offset `(n · channels + c) · 8`), little-endian; `channels` is the number of channels the stream holds and channel `i` is that set's `i`-th smallest logical channel (RM-3, RM-30). Its length is `size_bytes / (8 · channels)`, a whole number ≥ 1. The burst transmits on every channel of the stream. A repeated waveform's length must be at most `radio.tx.repeat_max_samples` and a multiple of `radio.tx.repeat_align_samples`. `TxBurst.metadata` is empty, or exactly the switch mark `radio.repeat_operation = "switch"` (RM-37). A burst whose region meets an accepted burst's is refused by RM-15.
  A radio Provider refuses a `TxBurst` with `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED`, and transmits nothing, when:
  - its target is not the transmit stream node;
  - the transmit stream holds no channel (RM-21);
  - `size_bytes` is not a positive multiple of `8 · channels`;
  - a sample component is not finite, where the Provider reads the waveform's bytes;
  - it repeats and its length is above `radio.tx.repeat_max_samples` or not a multiple of `radio.tx.repeat_align_samples`;
  - `metadata` is neither empty nor the switch mark (RM-37), or the mark is on a burst that does not repeat or whose late policy is not `send_asap_and_flag`;
  - its start, its end or, for a switch, its boundary (RM-37) does not fit the transmit SampleClock's root ticks in an `i64`, which TM-8 checks; or
  - its start is before the Provider's synchronisation end (the instant MR-9 and UR-13 state).

  A refusal is the Action's answer at receipt: the Run goes on, and the next Action is judged as if the refused one had not come.

  A burst whose waveform does not have the contract, the channel interleaving or the byte order above is not transmitted and is reported with `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED`.
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `mr_16_burst_refusals`, `mr_16_metadata_is_empty_or_the_mark_only`, `s15_parity_switch_mark_under_another_late_policy_is_refused_at_receipt`, `s16_parity_a_burst_time_that_does_not_fit_the_clock_is_refused_on_both_and_the_next_booking_is_served`, `mr_32_waveform_refusals`, `ur_21_txburst_refusals` and `rm_12_a_stream_node_names_its_direction_and_number`; the channel interleaving and the little-endian byte order by `mr_32_a_two_channel_waveform_is_channel_interleaved`; none named for a waveform refused for not having the `ezsdr.stream.cf32` contract.
- **RM-14** On receipt of a `TxBurst` a radio Provider evaluates `LatePolicy::decide(registry, at, now, device_lead)`, where `device_lead` is its **device lead** — its TimingEnvelope's `radio.timing.min_timed_command_lead_ns` less the delivery allowance it declares (MA-10), in `host.monotonic` (SC-27) — and `now` is its **decision instant**: the first transmit sample at or after the later of its current instant and its transmit clock's origin less that device lead, so that a target whose instant has passed is late even when it is the sample the current instant falls in, and a burst timed before its transmit clock's origin is late (RM-15). A stepped Provider's allowance is zero, so for MockRadio the device lead is the TimingEnvelope's lead. `OnTime`: the burst starts at `at`. `SendAsap`: the target moves to the first transmit sample at or after the decision instant plus the device lead that no accepted region holds (RM-15); if admitted, `radio.TIME_ERROR { cause: late, outcome: send_asap }` is emitted. A late `SendAsap` booking is placed past every accepted region it meets (RM-15). An untimed `TxBurst` (RS-19) takes RM-15's placement and no decision here.
  Under `SendAsap`, `BurstOpen.requested_target` is the burst's original `at`, or SC-23a's `requested_at` when admission already advanced it. If its Provider refuses that moved start, the burst is not transmitted and the Provider emits only `TIME_ERROR { outcome: refused }` plus `COMMAND_REJECTED` instead of a `send_asap` event. A head that the Provider moves again at its dispatch, because host lateness passed its start, is judged the same way against every accepted booking except itself, its joined pieces included; a joined piece that host lateness makes a late `send_asap` burst is refused the same way, since the record cannot move a joined piece. `Drop` and `PlanViolation`: the burst is not transmitted, and `radio.TIME_ERROR` with that outcome is emitted. The outcome reaches an admitted burst's `BurstRecord` through `BurstOpen.late` (SC-29a).
  *Producer obligation.*
  *Test:* `mr_17_a_target_whose_instant_has_passed_is_late_even_on_the_floor_sample`, `ur_21_a_burst_before_its_clock_s_origin_is_late`, `mr_16_burst_refusals`, `ur_21_first_dispatch_rechecks_all_late_policies`, `ur_21_a_late_joined_burst_cannot_move_and_is_refused`, `v58_09_a_pong_short_of_the_lead_is_late_as_on_hardware`, `rm_14_the_recorded_requested_target_is_the_requested_at` and `rm_14_the_handed_burst_keeps_its_requested_at` (SC-23a's `requested_at` kept as `requested_target` on time and after a `SendAsap` move, MockRadio and the UHD Module).
- **RM-15** Bursts on one transmit stream are held in target order. A burst ends at the end of its waveform (without `repeat`), at the start of the next burst, at a `Stop` for its stream or device, or at the Run's stop, whichever is first; a repeated burst ends only at one of the last three. A booking is decided when it is received, in receipt order: a booking whose region meets an accepted booking's region is refused with `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED`, so the one received second is refused.
  The one exception is a booking whose configure the device refuses: it leaves the record, and a surviving head of at most 32 samples is closed at its standalone front, so no later booking joins it. The pieces joined behind a head that leaves keep their regions (the chain's front, and each piece's front as a standalone head), and no booking joins their chain. A region is `[start, front)`: a standalone closed device burst of 1 to 32 transmitted samples (1 to 3 padded to four) fronts at its padded end plus `g`, `g = max(3, ceil(15 µs × the stream's sample rate))` samples (15 at 1 MS/s, 30 at 2, 3000 at 200), and a head longer than 32 samples or a joined piece at its end plus four; a repeat's region runs until the `Stop` that ends it (RM-16). A booking starting at exactly the end of a chain of more than 32 samples joins it, and is accepted, when it is received no later than its start less the device lead and the delivery allowance (the Provider's profile value; MR-3 for MockRadio); a later receipt is refused. A booking that starts before an accepted chain is refused too when the Provider may already have handed that chain to the device: the chain starts before `at_or_after(now + W)`, the hand-over horizon (`W` is `radio.timing.tx_in_flight_ns`), and no chain that ends at or before its start still accepts a join received at `now` (`J(e)`). A `SendAsap` move never starts before such a chain. Every block a Provider transmits goes through the Kernel's `BurstTracker` (SC-29).
  A configure that returns `lost: true` is not a refusal: its booking keeps its region.
  Lateness (RM-14) is decided before the footprint. An accepted booking's region is its **footprint**: its samples and its own device close, up to its front (above); a finite booking that UR-22 abandoned keeps it, a repeat that UR-22 abandoned keeps it up to its cursor plus five, and a chain that a `Stop` cuts keeps the region RM-16 leaves it. A booking that is not late and meets a footprint is refused at receipt. A booking delayed only by the region a `Stop` leaves where it cancelled bookings is moved or refused as RM-16 states; one delayed by host lateness or a recovery at dispatch is judged by its late policy there (RM-14). An untimed booking is placed at the first free sample. An untimed booking starts at the first sample from which its region meets no accepted region, no Stop region and no chain that the hand-over rule above forbids it to start before, at or after `max(at, ordinary)`, never earlier; it carries no `TIME_ERROR`, and is refused only when an open repeat holds the rest of the stream. The late policy does not apply to it.
  A burst never starts before its transmit clock's origin: a target before it is late, and the burst's `LatePolicy` applies — `send_asap` moves it to the origin with `TIME_ERROR { send_asap }`, `drop` drops it. A burst timed before its transmit clock's origin meets this (a Session burst with no `at` does not: RS-19 admits it no earlier than that origin); its Provider decides such a burst as if at its clock's origin less its device lead.
  A Provider that hands a burst's samples to a device ahead of their instants, and cannot recall them, cannot start the next burst before the first sample it has not yet handed over. Its late bound is the later of RM-14's bound and that same-clock front; equality is on time. The Provider's spec states the in-flight window `radio.timing.tx_in_flight_ns`. When that window is nonzero, the device burst closes with an EOB send of at least two samples per channel: a one-sample payload close adds one zero, or three when it is a device burst's first send (UR-22 pads that to four), and a close with no payload sends two zeros. The next safe start is the closing send's exclusive end plus `g` samples for a standalone closed device burst of at most 32 transmitted samples, plus three otherwise (a head longer than 32 samples closes with its held tail and at most one zero, so its front is its payload end plus four; a two-zero close ends two past the cursor, so its front is the cursor plus five). A Provider arms a stream's record with `g` where the stream's clock is registered (the rate is fixed for a Run, RM-27); a record not armed refuses a finite short head. A zero window preserves the ordinary behavior.

### Receive faults

- **RM-17** An **overrun** at `f` loses every receive sample in `[f, f + radio.timing.overflow_restart_gap_ns)`. A block without a jump consumes its pending reports without an event; a report with no following block produces no event. A jump with no report produces no event. An overrun the Provider causes by dropping a block its link refused is emitted when it drops it, outside this rule (SC-20a).

### The RF safety envelope

- **RM-19** The section `radio.rf_envelope` is this document (`RfEnvelope`, `deny_unknown_fields`):

  ```text
  RfEnvelope { allowed_bands: [{ lo_hz: num, hi_hz: num }],          required; lo_hz ≤ hi_hz
               max_gain_db:   optional num,                          applies to every transmit channel
               tx_enabled:    optional bool | [bool],                absent: every transmit channel enabled
               antenna_ports: optional [string] }                    absent: any antenna
  ```

  `RfEnvelopeCheck` runs at `validate`, `prepare` and `runtime`, and for each fragment `F`, over `effective[F]` overlaid with `proposed[F]` (SB-30), reports one `Violation { check: radio.rf_envelope, key, requested, reason }` — `reason` beginning `"RM-19: <fragment>: "` — for each violation RM-33 defines.
  1. the section does not parse as `RfEnvelope`, or a band has `lo_hz > hi_hz` (one violation, `key: None`, for the whole check);
  2. a layout configuration violates a transmit-channel, receive-antenna or transmit-enable limit (RM-33).

  Receive frequencies are not limited. A numeric value is read as `Int` or `Num`; one of another kind is a violation of its key.

  A receive channel's antenna is judged at the `runtime` point when it changes on a stopped stream (RM-21); a receive frequency outside the board's range is refused by `coerce` (RM-8). A transmit channel's frequency, gain and antenna have the update class `cold` (RM-21), so a Run-time change of one reaches the `runtime` point, where a value outside the envelope is refused (RM-19).
  *Test:* `rm_19_rf_envelope_cases`; the value outside the envelope refused at connect and the Run-time change refused by the envelope check (`radio.rf_envelope`) by `v58_16_a_value_outside_the_rf_envelope_is_refused_at_connect` and `v58_16_a_transmit_value_outside_the_rf_envelope_is_refused_on_an_empty_stream`, and the receive antenna outside the envelope refused on a stopped stream by `v58_16_a_receive_antenna_outside_the_rf_envelope_is_refused_on_a_stopped_stream`.

### Update classes of radio keys

- **RM-21** A channel's `frequency_hz`, `gain_db` and `antenna` have the update class `cold` (RM-27) in both directions; no other radio key has one: the Kernel refuses a Session's change of a channel's membership or of a rate, and a Spec schedule of a key with no class (RS-52, SB-16); a schedule of a channel value passes and the Provider refuses it, for it carries an instant. A receive stream is **on** from the Run's start until a `Stop` of `<device>/rx/s<k>` or `<device>`, and again from a `start_rx` (RM-12); it **runs** while it is on, holds a channel and has a link, and is not halted by a refusal (RM-25). A receive stream is **streaming** when its line, replayed over every command booked so far in the order they take effect (RM-25), ends on: a booked `start_rx` makes it so at its effective instant, a `Stop` booked later does not cancel it, and a start the device refuses leaves it so.
  A Provider **stages** a `cold` value when it carries no instant, its channel is in a stream, and the stream takes it: a receive stream that is not streaming, or a transmit stream whose busy end (below) is not after the value's receipt. A later value for the key replaces an earlier one, and `Stop`, a loss and `Provider::stop` clear nothing. Otherwise it emits `radio.COMMAND_REJECTED` naming the cause, the rule and the Action's id, and does not use the value (the Kernel's configuration keeps it, KC-27). A transmit stream's busy end is the exclusive end of its last booked burst; an open repeat has none until a `Stop` takes effect at `s`, after which the end is `min(busy end, B)`, with `B` the Stop bound of RM-16. A burst refused after its booking stays counted to its booked end. A transmit burst takes a copy of its stream's staged values and their generation (a count of the stream's accepted transmit values) when it is booked; a device whose configuration holds another generation is configured before the burst, and an equal value configures again (note 47 stage 4). A Provider refuses a `start_rx` of a receive stream that is streaming the same way (kind `command`, the rule and the Action's id) and its line is unchanged, so a booked start is never revised to an earlier origin: `Stop` first, then `start_rx`. A stream that holds no channel is never streaming.
  A `start_rx` takes a copy of the staged values when it is booked, and its segment begins with them. Another stream's state does not matter. A stream holds the set of channels whose `radio.d.c<n>.stream` names it (RM-27); it holds a channel exactly when that set is not empty. A `Stop`, a `start_rx` or a loss changes whether a receive stream runs; its segment ends (RM-16) and, if it runs afterwards, the next one starts (RM-25). A transmit stream has a SampleClock while it holds a channel; a `Stop` ends its bursts, not its clock.
  *Test:* `rm_21_*` (`crates/ezsdr-radio/tests/staging.rs`), `rm_21_streaming_is_the_final_state_of_the_replayed_line`, `rm_26_the_staging_oracle_agrees_with_staging` (values and starts), and per Provider `mr_18_*` (spec 09) and `ur_26_*` (spec 18); the transmit form by `rm_21_a_transmit_value_at_the_exclusive_end_is_accepted_and_one_sample_before_refused`, `rm_21_a_stop_bounds_an_open_repeat_by_the_in_flight_window` and `ur_25_a_burst_whose_snapshot_equals_the_device_s_makes_no_apply`; the busy end an abandon leaves to a booking accepted after a `Stop` by `e2a_80_a_failed_send_after_a_stop_leaves_the_booking_accepted_after_it`, `e2a_80_the_booking_accepted_after_the_stop_is_the_playing_repeat` and `txbook_abandon_keeps_a_later_booking_its_chain_playing_and_busy_end`.

### Hot-path forms

- **RM-24** `radio.RX_OVERFLOW` has a hot-path form of 9 bytes, little-endian: byte 0 the cause (`0` overrun, `1` sequence), bytes 1–8 `lost` as u64. `RxOverflowPayload::to_hot(&self) -> [u8; 9]` writes it. `RxOverflowPayload::from_hot(bytes: &[u8]) -> Result<RxOverflowPayload, String>` reads it and refuses another length or a cause byte other than 0 or 1. `RxOverflowPayload::from_payload(payload: &serde_json::Value) -> Result<RxOverflowPayload, String>` reads a delivered event's payload in either form — RM-11's `{ cause, lost }` object or the array of byte values the Kernel's drain makes of a hot-path record (RS-34) — and refuses anything else, including the former object with `restart_gap_ns` and the former 17-byte array. `RxOverflowHotPayload`, the array as a Manifest holds it, has the committed schema `schemas/radio/rx_overflow_hot_payload.v1.json` (PO-7), so a reader that validates payloads has a schema for both forms. ### The lattice

- **RM-25** Every SampleClock's origin is on its root's lattice — a whole multiple of the numerator, in lowest terms, of its `root_ticks_per_tick` — so clocks of one ratio share every sample instant and T0 is a sample instant of every stream. The first receive segment starts at T0 (MR-11, UR-15), the first transmit clock at the first lattice instant at or after `arm` (MR-9). A receive segment whose device misses its timed start keeps its origin: its first block carries `GAP_BEFORE` with `lost` its index (SC-13), so an index still names `origin + index · root_ticks_per_tick`. A later receive segment, which only a `start_rx` after a `Stop` begins, starts at the first lattice instant at or after its effective instant, the stream's previous cut plus `radio.timing.rx_start_lead_ns` and the instant the Provider is ready for it plus that lead; the Provider's spec states its ready instant, and a Mock profile emulating a device states that device's. After a cut, readiness includes one receive call at the old rate and the delivery allowance; the start lead follows those terms.
  Commands and faults of one stream take effect in the order of their effective instants and, at one instant, in the order received, faults — a device loss among them — counting as received at the Run's start; `Provider::stop` is a command and counts in the order it was received. A `start_rx` takes effect at its `at` or its receipt and never before the stream's start (RM-12). The start lead (`rx_start_lead_ns`) runs from the instant the Provider is ready and covers the start's configure: when the configure ends with less than the declared command lead (`min_timed_command_lead_ns`, RM-6) before the planned origin, the start keeps the origin and the Provider reports `radio.START_LEAD_SHORT` (`ezsdr_radio::timeline::lead_left`); a configure that ends at or after the origin is the missed start above. MockRadio's configure takes no time and never reports it. Between a cut and the next origin nothing is delivered and nothing is a gap.
  A stream's channel set and its rate are fixed for the Run (RM-21), and a stream whose set is empty begins no segment; a segment's frequency, gain and antenna are the values its `start_rx` took (RM-21).
  A receive SampleClock is registered with the first block its segment publishes and ended at its cut, so a segment that publishes none — one cut at or before its origin, or one whose samples were all lost before a loss or an abort — has none; a transmit clock is registered at `arm`, so that a burst admitted after it can target it (KC-21a).
  A planned event refused or pre-empted at its instant — a configuration the device does not apply, a loss, `Provider::stop` — is removed; cuts made and clocks ended stand, and only the events after that instant are recomputed, a later one the new plan refuses being refused then (`COMMAND_REJECTED`). A refused `start_rx` — one whose configuration the device does not apply — **halts** the stream: it has no segment until a `Stop` and the next `start_rx` (a halted stream is streaming, RM-21), which starts it by the rule above; the refused value stays the configuration of record (KC-27). MockRadio refuses a `start_rx` on a streaming stream (RM-21) and one whose booking the line cannot represent, each with the Action's id.
  A receive segment that a refused `start_rx` would have begun counts, for planning, as running until the next `Stop` or end cuts it, whatever `delivered` holds; that cut is the previous cut of the start rule above. The segment is not in the plan: its owner does not begin it, and it has no clock and no record. The stream halts as before.

- **RM-37** A `TxBurst` with `repeat: true`, late policy `send_asap_and_flag` and the switch mark (RM-13) is a **switch**: it replaces the repeat playing on its transmit stream. A repeat is playing when it has started and no `Stop`, loss or `Provider::stop` has ended it; a switch is refused when no repeat is playing (a finite burst, a stopped or unstarted repeat), another switch is pending or its boundary does not fit the clock (RM-13), and a Provider may refuse one whose repeat has not yet started on its device (UR-38). Its boundary `b` is the first sample `origin + j · N` of the playing repeat (origin, period `N`) at or after the later of the burst's `at` and the receipt's sample plus `radio.timing.tx_in_flight_ns` and the device lead (RM-14); it is chosen at receipt and never moved. A timed switch whose `b` is later than the first such sample at or after its `at` alone records `at` as its new repeat's requested target (SC-29a); one at that sample records none.
  The switch is pending until it is settled by the sample at `b`, the first of the new waveform, which the Provider sends or emits in one operation, the *boundary send*. It is `applied` once the Provider took that sample (the send whole, or short with at least one sample taken) and device time has reached `b`, or a Stop or `Provider::stop` whose bound `B` (RM-16) is after `b` follows it, since what is handed is not recalled (RM-16), unless a loss at a sample at or before `b` follows before device time reaches `b` (UHD leaves the switch pending until then; MockRadio settles it at the Stop, MR-42): the old repeat ends at `b` and the new waveform begins with its own length as its period. It is `cancelled` when a Stop, a loss or `Provider::stop` comes before the sample at `b` was taken, when `B` is at or before `b`, when a loss comes at a sample at or before `b` (a loss settles by device time: the sample at `b` was never played), and when a Provider that ends the repeat on its own (an underflow, an expired timeline or a failed send it abandons) does so before handing the sample at `b`; no sample of the new waveform is then sent. It is `uncertain` when the boundary send ended in an exception, so that whether the device took any of it is unknown, unless `B` is at or before `b`; the exception wins over a loss at or before `b`.
  A Stop or a loss that comes while the boundary send is unresolved leaves the switch pending until its result, unless its bound `B` is at or before `b`, which cancels the switch at once. The result then settles it, except that a whole result under a Stop that came before device time reached `b` keeps it pending until then, as above.
  A switch whose boundary send has no result when the Provider writes its Manifest is settled as one whose send ended in an exception; one that a Stop left pending before device time reached `b` is `applied` there.
